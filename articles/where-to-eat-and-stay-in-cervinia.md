## Why go

<p class="lede">When it is December, the Apennines are brown and the Dolomites are running on snowmaking, Cervinia is white.</p>

The lifts climb to about 3,500 metres, the highest skiing in Italy. The runs are long, wide and gentle, built for cruising, and you can ski over the border into Switzerland for lunch. The village itself is no beauty. You will not be looking at it. You will be looking up at the Matterhorn.

## A perfect day

<ol class="dayplan">
<li><span class="when">Morning</span><span class="what">Straight up to Plateau Rosa while the snow is firm, and the long runs back down.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Lunch</span><span class="what"><a href="#etoile">Chalet Etoile</a> at Plan Maison, on the terrace, facing the Matterhorn. Or ski across to Zermatt if you have the international pass.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Afternoon</span><span class="what">The easy cruising runs above the village while the light turns gold on the mountain.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Evening</span><span class="what">Five minutes down the valley to <a href="#neiges">Les Neiges d'Antan</a> for dinner.</span></li>
</ol>

## Before you go

<ul class="tips">
<li>The Italian-side pass is 64 euro. Add Zermatt and it is 88.</li>
<li>Wind closes the top lifts more often than snow does. Check before you go up.</li>
<li>Without a car: train to Châtillon-Saint-Vincent, then a bus up the valley.</li>
</ul>

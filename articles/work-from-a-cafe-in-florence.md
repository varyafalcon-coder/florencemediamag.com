## The real problem: nobody tells you the rules

Most Italian bars are designed for a coffee at the counter that takes ninety seconds. There is no sign saying laptops are welcome and no sign saying they are not. So you sit down, open your computer, and spend the next hour reading the barista's face.

The anxiety is usually unnecessary. Florence has a large student population and a growing number of people working remotely, and staff in most places have seen it a hundred times. What annoys them is not the laptop. It is taking a four-seat table alone at one in the afternoon, ordering one espresso in three hours, or unplugging a lamp to charge a phone.

The short version: avoid the lunch rush between roughly 12:30 and 14:30, order again every hour or so, and take the smallest table you can. Do that and you can work almost anywhere in this city.

## Where working on a laptop does not feel strange

### Biblioteca delle Oblate

Via dell'Oriuolo 24. Open Tuesday to Friday 9:00 to 19:00, Wednesday to Friday until 23:00, Saturday 10:00 to 23:00, Monday 14:00 to 19:00, closed Sunday.

A public library in a former fourteenth century convent two minutes from the Duomo. Free to enter, quiet by design, full of students working through long days. The reading rooms are the serious option; the rooftop cafe has a view straight onto Brunelleschi's dome and a much looser atmosphere.

This is the only place on the list where nobody could think a laptop is odd, because that is what the building is for. It fills up. Weekday afternoons in term time are the worst, mornings are reliable.

### Ditta Artigianale, Via dello Sprone

Via dello Sprone 5/R. Open daily 7:30 to 19:00.

The Oltrarno branch of the roaster that brought specialty coffee to Florence. The ground floor is a busy bar. The upstairs room is the reason it is here: armchairs, tables, a calmer atmosphere that people already use for work.

Full at brunch, so this is a morning or late afternoon place. There is a small charge for table service, standard in Italy and worth knowing before the bill arrives.

### Ditta Artigianale, Piazza Ferrucci

Lungarno Benvenuto Cellini 1r. Open daily 8:00 to 21:30.

Everyone calls this one Piazza Ferrucci, but the registered address is on Lungarno Cellini, so search for that if your map argues with you. About twenty minutes east along the river from the centre, which is exactly why it works: fewer visitors, more locals, a garden terrace set back from the road and a lot of indoor seating. Open until 21:30, so it is the one option here for people who work in the evening.

### Todo Modo

Via dei Fossi 15R. Open Monday to Saturday 10:00 to 19:30, closed Sunday.

A bookshop with a cafe and small bistro at the back, between Santa Maria Novella and the river. Independent, calm, staffed by people entirely used to customers sitting for a while.

It is small. Tables are close together, and if you need to spread out or take a call this is not the place. For quiet solo work with a coffee, in the morning, it is one of the nicest rooms in central Florence.

### Shake Cafe, Manifattura Tabacchi

Piazza Francesca Morvillo 13. Open Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00, weekends 10:00 to 17:00.

Manifattura Tabacchi is a former tobacco factory northwest of the centre, redeveloped into a campus of studios and workspaces. The cafe inside is large, bright, air conditioned and full of people who work in the buildings around it.

The most obviously laptop-friendly place on the list and the least central: roughly twenty five minutes on foot or a short tram ride from Santa Maria Novella. Closes at 17:00, so daytime only.

## Where not to open a laptop

Not a rule, just what will make your life easier.

Any bar directly on Piazza del Duomo, Piazza della Signoria or Ponte Vecchio. Turnover is the entire business model. You will not be thrown out, but you will feel it.

A trattoria at lunch. These are restaurants with limited covers, and a laptop on a set table during service is genuinely inconvenient for them.

A standing-only bar with three stools. If there is no table, there is no seat for your laptop either.

## And you can always just ask

"Posso lavorare qui un po'?" almost always gets a yes, and it changes the whole dynamic of the visit.

## How to choose

From Florence the choice is really about time. The Apennines are close enough for a day out and cheap, but they are low mountains and the snow comes and goes. The Alps are four to five hours away, need a night at least, and in exchange give you terrain that goes on for days and snow you can count on.

If you have one free Saturday, go to Abetone. If you have a long weekend, go to the Dolomites or to Pontedilegno-Tonale. If it is December and nothing is white, go high: Cervinia or Livigno.

## Up to two hours: day trips

All three of these sit on the ridge between Tuscany and Emilia, between 1,300 and 2,000 metres. On a good winter they are excellent value. On a warm one they open late and close early, so this is the part of the article where the lift status page matters most.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Abetone and Val di Luce</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About 90 km, an hour and fifty minutes by car via Pistoia and the old Modena road</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 25 runs and 17 lifts across Abetone, Val di Luce, Ovovia and Pulicchio, with Doganaccia above Cutigliano on a separate pass</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,400 to 1,900 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>51 euro on weekdays last season. The 2026/27 season pass is 790 euro</dd></div>
<div><dt>Season</dt><dd>Planned 4 December 2026 to 11 April 2027, snow permitting</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>A first day on skis, families, a Saturday with friends</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

Abetone is where Florentines learned to ski, and it still feels like that: a village on a pass, wooden hotels, children in ski school, very little pretence. Val di Luce on the far side is the newer, wider bowl and usually holds the snow best.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Corno alle Scale</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About two hours by car, via Pistoia and Porretta Terme to Lizzano in Belvedere</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>About 15 runs on the north face of the Corno, inside a regional park</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,350 to 1,950 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>On the last published list, 28 euro on weekdays and 38 at weekends</dd></div>
<div><dt>Season</dt><dd>Planned 5 December 2026 to 29 March 2027</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>A cheap, quiet day. It is small, but the setting is the wildest of the three</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

The Corno is Bologna's mountain more than Florence's, which is why on a Tuscan weekend it can be the emptiest of the three. The runs are short and the lifts are not new. The view from the top, across the whole Apennine ridge, is the best in this section.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Cimone</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>Around two hours fifteen by car, to Sestola or Montecreto</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 50 km of runs across Passo del Lupo, Pian del Falco, Lago della Ninfa, Cimoncino and Montecreto</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>Up to about 2,100 metres, close to the summit of Monte Cimone</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>44 euro on weekdays, 57 at weekends and over Christmas, plus a 3 euro card</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>The most skiing you can do in a day from Florence, and the best food on the slopes</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

The Cimone is the biggest ski area in the Apennines and the highest, which is why people from Bologna and Modena come here rather than to Tuscany. It is the one Apennine resort where a full day does not feel like laps of the same three runs.

## Four to five hours: weekends

This is where the skiing changes category. Every area in this section has more terrain than you can cover in two days, and snowmaking on almost every run. Leave on Friday after work, or on Saturday at six.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Val Gardena</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About four hours by car via the A22, exit Chiusa. Or train to Bolzano, then bus 350 up to Ortisei</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 175 km with Alpe di Siusi, on the Sellaronda loop, inside the 1,200 km of Dolomiti Superski</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,200 to 2,500 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>80 euro a day, 89 in high season. Buying online two days ahead takes 5% off</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>A first Dolomites trip, and the one that works without a car</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

The train to Bolzano and the bus up the valley take about the same time as the drive and nobody has to do the A22 in the dark. Ortisei has the most life in the evening. Selva, further up, is where you want to be if the plan is to ski the Sellaronda.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Madonna di Campiglio</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About four hours fifteen by car via Trento. Or train to Trento and a bus up</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 150 km: Spinale, Grostè and Pradalago, linked to Pinzolo and Folgarida-Marilleva</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 850 to 2,500 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>75 euro in low season, 87 in high, 89 at peak dates</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>Long, well groomed red runs and a smart village where the lifts start a short walk from the centre</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

Campiglio faces the Brenta Dolomites, which are a different shape from the ones above Cortina: sharper, darker, closer.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Pontedilegno-Tonale</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About four and a half hours by car, via Brescia and Val Camonica or via Trento and Val di Sole. Or train to Brescia, the local line to Edolo and a bus up</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>About 100 km and around 30 lifts, linking Temù, Ponte di Legno, Passo del Tonale and the Presena glacier</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,120 to 3,000 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>From 52 euro a day, 70 at peak dates (26 December to 10 January, and February). A 10 euro discount applies on most other days</dd></div>
<div><dt>Season</dt><dd>Late November to early May, with the glacier usually among the first runs in Italy to open</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>Value. Alpine size and a glacier at 3,000 metres for less than the Dolomites</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

This is the area people from Milan and Brescia know and Florentines mostly do not. It has two characters. Passo del Tonale, at 1,880 metres, is a ski station built for skiing, with wide easy runs and snow from November to May. Ponte di Legno, below it, is a real stone and wood village with a church square, shops and evenings. The two are joined by lifts, and the run from the Presena glacier down to the valley is one of the longest in the Alps.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Cortina d'Ampezzo</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About four and a half hours by car. Without one, train to Venice Mestre or Calalzo, then bus</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 120 km: Tofana, Faloria, Cinque Torri and Lagazuoi, inside Dolomiti Superski</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,220 to 2,930 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>80 euro a day, 89 in high season</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>Scenery, and a town that is an event in itself</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

Cortina hosted the women's alpine races at the 2026 Winter Olympics, and the Tofana slopes are the ones you saw on television. The town is expensive and knows it. The skiing is still some of the most beautiful anywhere, especially at Cinque Torri, where you ski past rock towers and First World War trenches. The areas are not all linked, so expect a bus or two.

## Five hours and more: the high Alps

These are the answer to a warm December, when the Apennines are brown and the lower Alps are running on snowmaking. Worth it for three days or more, not for two.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Breuil-Cervinia</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About five hours by car. Or train to Châtillon-Saint-Vincent and a bus up the valley</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 150 km on the Italian side with Valtournenche, about 360 km with Zermatt</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,520 to 3,480 metres, the highest skiing in Italy</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>64 euro on the Italian side, 88 including Zermatt</dd></div>
<div><dt>Season</dt><dd>Planned opening 24 October 2026, one of the earliest in Europe</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>Early and late season, long gentle runs, lunch in Switzerland</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

The village itself is no beauty. Look up at the Matterhorn instead.

<div class="venue">
<h3>Livigno</h3>
<dl class="venue-meta">
<div><dt>From Florence</dt><dd>About five and a half hours by car, the last part over a high pass. Snow chains in the car are a requirement, not a suggestion</dd></div>
<div><dt>Size</dt><dd>Around 115 km and 30 lifts on two sides of the valley, Mottolino and Carosello 3000</dd></div>
<div><dt>Altitude</dt><dd>About 1,800 to 2,800 metres</dd></div>
<div><dt>Lift pass</dt><dd>51.50 euro in the early and late weeks (28 November to 18 December, and from 10 April), 66 in season, 74.50 in high season</dd></div>
<div><dt>Best for</dt><dd>Snowboarders, snow from November to May, and duty-free shopping in the evening</dd></div>
</dl>
</div>

Livigno is a long, high valley that is cold all winter, which is the whole point. It is the furthest place in this article and the one most likely to have good snow when nowhere else does.

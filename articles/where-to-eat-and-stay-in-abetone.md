## Why go

<p class="lede">This is where Florentines learned to ski, and it still feels like it.</p>

A village on a pass at 1,400 metres, wooden hotels, ski schools full of Tuscan children and very little pretence. Val di Luce, on the far side, is the newer, wider bowl and usually holds the snow best. Weekdays are nearly empty. Saturdays are a Florentine outing, with the whole city on the slopes.

You do not come for size, you come because you can wake up in Florence and be skiing by ten. And because fifteen minutes down the road is one of the best trattorias in the Tuscan mountains.

## A perfect day

<ol class="dayplan">
<li><span class="when">7:00</span><span class="what">Leave Florence via Pistoia and the old Modena road. Be at the lifts before the Saturday crowd.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Morning</span><span class="what">Start in Val di Luce, where the snow is usually best, then cross back towards the village.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Lunch</span><span class="what">Keep it light on the slopes. The real meal comes later.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">Late afternoon</span><span class="what">Drive fifteen minutes down to Cutigliano for an early dinner at <a href="#fagiolino">Trattoria da Fagiolino</a>, then home.</span></li>
</ol>

## Before you go

<ul class="tips">
<li>Check the lift status the evening before. Abetone depends on natural snow and snowmaking, and a warm winter means a late start.</li>
<li>Go on a weekday if you can. Saturdays bring half of Florence.</li>
<li>There is no practical way to get here without a car.</li>
</ul>

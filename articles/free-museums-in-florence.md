Florence's big museums cost real money, and two or three of them in a short trip add up. Once a month they cost nothing.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>The first Sunday of the month is free at the state museums.</strong> The Uffizi, the Accademia, Palazzo Pitti, the Bargello, the Medici Chapels, San Marco and others. No booking: arrive before opening.</p>
</div>

## How the first Sunday works

Entry is free for everyone at the state museums, first come, first served. You cannot reserve, so the lines at the Uffizi and the Accademia start well before opening. Pick one big museum, be there early, and spend the rest of the day at smaller ones, where the free Sunday makes a calm visit even better value.

The Bargello and San Marco are the smart choices: world-class, free, and with a fraction of the queue.

On the same Sunday, the city's civic museums, such as Palazzo Vecchio, are free only for residents of the Florence metropolitan area.

## Free every day

Plenty of Florence costs nothing at any time.

- **The Loggia dei Lanzi**, an open-air sculpture gallery on Piazza della Signoria, with Cellini's Perseus and Giambologna's Rape of the Sabine Women.
- **San Miniato al Monte**, the Romanesque church above Piazzale Michelangelo, with the best view in the city and Gregorian chant at vespers.
- **Most churches**, including Santo Spirito, Brunelleschi's last great building.
- **The Giardino delle Rose**, a rose garden on the hillside below Piazzale Michelangelo, free and open from spring.
- **The outside of the Duomo**, which is the point anyway.

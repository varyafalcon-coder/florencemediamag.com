Every city in Italy has its saint's day, and Florence's is 24 June, the feast of San Giovanni, John the Baptist. Offices close, Florentines stay in town, and the day ends with fireworks over the river.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Book Calcio Storico in spring, or just come for the fireworks.</strong> The match is ticketed and sells out. The fireworks are free and the whole city watches them.</p>
</div>

## Calcio Storico

A game first played in the sixteenth century, now a brutal mix of football, rugby and wrestling on a pitch of sand laid in Piazza Santa Croce. Four teams, one for each historic quarter: the Whites of Santo Spirito, the Blues of Santa Croce, the Greens of San Giovanni and the Reds of Santa Maria Novella.

The two semi-finals are played in mid-June and the final on 24 June, in the late afternoon, around six. Before each match a historical parade in Renaissance costume, drums and flags winds through the centre to the square. The parade is free to watch from the street.

Tickets go on sale online through TicketOne, usually in spring, with a limit of a few per person. Many seats go to the quarters' own members, so the public allocation is small.

## The fireworks

At about ten at night the *fochi di San Giovanni* light up the sky over the Arno, fired from the area around Piazzale Michelangelo and the river. The Lungarni, the bridges and the riverbanks near San Niccolo are where everyone stands. Arrive early; the bridges fill up an hour before.

## The day itself

24 June is a holiday in Florence only. Some shops and offices are closed, while museums usually stay open. It is a good day to be in the city and see it belong to the Florentines.

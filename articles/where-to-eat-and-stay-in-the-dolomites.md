## Which valley

<p class="lede">Lunch is the centre of a ski day in the Dolomites, not a break from it. Choose the valley first, then the hut.</p>

<ul class="tips">
<li><strong>Val Gardena</strong> if it is your first time, or you want to arrive by train. Wooden Ladin villages, the Sellaronda loop, huts with real cooking.</li>
<li><strong>Cortina</strong> if you want the scenery and the town. Olympic slopes, rock towers, the evening walk on Corso Italia. Expensive and proud of it.</li>
<li><strong>Madonna di Campiglio</strong> if you like everything well kept: groomed red runs, a smart village, lifts a short walk from the centre.</li>
</ul>

## Cortina d'Ampezzo

Host of the women's alpine races at the 2026 Olympics, and the most beautiful skiing on this page, especially at Cinque Torri, where you ski past rock towers and First World War trenches. The areas are not all linked, so expect a bus or two.

## What to order

<dl class="dishes">
<div><dt>Canederli</dt><dd>Bread dumplings with speck or cheese, in broth or with melted butter.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Schlutzkrapfen</dt><dd>Half-moon pasta filled with spinach and ricotta.</dd></div>

## Before you go

<ul class="tips">
<li>Book the famous huts a day or two ahead in high season, and arrive before 12:30.</li>
<li>Buying the Dolomiti Superski pass online two days ahead takes 5% off.</li>
<li>Without a car: Val Gardena by train to Bolzano, Madonna di Campiglio by train to Trento, Cortina by train to Venice Mestre or Calalzo, then bus.</li>
</ul>

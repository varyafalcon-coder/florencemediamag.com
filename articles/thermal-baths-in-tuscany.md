## Why go in winter?

<p class="lede">Hot water comes out of the ground all over southern Tuscany, and the coldest months are the best time to sit in it.</p>

The volcanic ground around Monte Amiata heats springs that the Etruscans and Romans already used. Some run free through woods and over limestone; others fill thermal pools you pay to enter. On a clear winter morning the steam rises off the water and the summer crowds are gone.

## Before you go

<ul class="tips">
<li>Take off silver jewellery. Sulphur turns it black.</li>
<li>Bring water shoes to the free springs. Limestone and tufa are slippery.</li>
<li>The free springs fill up on summer weekends. Go early, late, or in winter.</li>
<li>Parking near Saturnia and Bagni San Filippo is paid. Keep coins or a parking app ready.</li>
<li>Do not climb on the white formations. They take centuries to grow.</li>
<li>No bathing in the square at Bagno Vignoni. It is a monument.</li>
</ul>

Florence is the centre of a web of railway lines, and half of Tuscany is within an hour or so. You do not need a car for any of these.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Regional trains need no booking.</strong> Fixed fares, buy on the day at the station or in the Trenitalia app, and validate a paper ticket before boarding.</p>
</div>

## Pisa, about an hour

Regional trains run often from Santa Maria Novella, around 8 to 9 euro. From Pisa Centrale it is a thirty-minute walk across the city to the Piazza dei Miracoli, or a short bus ride. Book the tower climb ahead.

## Lucca, about an hour and a quarter

Around 8 euro by regional train. The station is just outside the walls. Hire a bike and ride the four-kilometre circuit on top of them, then eat at a trattoria inside. Lucca and Pisa are close enough to combine in one long day.

## Bologna, 35 to 40 minutes

By high-speed train, from around 10 euro booked ahead and more on the day. A different city altogether: arcades, towers and the best food in Italy, by its own account.

## Arezzo and Pistoia, under an hour

Two cities most visitors skip. Arezzo, 45 minutes to an hour, has Piero della Francesca's frescoes in San Francesco and a huge antiques fair on the first weekend of each month. Pistoia, 30 to 45 minutes, has a beautiful cathedral square and almost no tourists.

## Siena, by bus

The train to Siena takes about an hour and a half and leaves you at the bottom of the hill. The direct bus from Florence is usually quicker and stops near the centre. Arrive for lunch, walk to the Campo, climb the Torre del Mangia.

## Volterra and Livorno

Volterra, on its hill, is a bus or a car trip. Livorno, on the coast, is about an hour and a quarter by train, for the sea and the fish.

Aperitivo is the hour before dinner when Italians have a drink and a bite. In Milan it means a cocktail and a buffet. In Florence it is mostly wine, often standing up, with something on bread.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Order a Negroni at least once.</strong> It was invented in Florence around 1920, when Count Camillo Negroni asked for gin instead of soda in his Americano.</p>
</div>

## Wine bars

The real Florentine aperitivo: a glass from a small producer and a crostino, in a room that holds a dozen people. Fiaschetteria Nuvoli near the Duomo, an old wine shop from the 1950s, is worth a stop too.

## Santo Spirito at sunset

The square in front of Santo Spirito is the city's living room on warm evenings: bars around the edge, people sitting on the church steps with a glass. Go around seven, and start at Volume, a bar in an old hat-maker's workshop on the square.

## The wine windows

Look for small arched hatches in the walls of old palaces, about the size of a book. These are the *buchette del vino*, through which noble families once sold wine from their estates directly onto the street. Most are closed up. A few have been reopened, and Babae on Via di Santo Spirito still pours a glass through its window for an hour each evening.

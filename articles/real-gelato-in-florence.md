## The three rules

**Look at the containers.** Real gelato is stored in covered metal tins, called pozzetti, or in flat trays under a lid. Gelato piled into peaks in an open display has been stabilised with vegetable fat and emulsifiers so it holds that shape under lights. The peaks are marketing, and they cost you texture.

**Look at the banana.** Banana oxidises and turns grey, in a tub exactly as it does on a plate. If it is bright yellow, it is coloured.

**Look at the pistachio.** Real pistachio is a dull khaki brown-green. Bright mint green is dye. The same test works on mint: real mint gelato made with leaves comes out off-white, not toothpaste blue.

## What else gives it away

A flavour list of forty or fifty means somebody is buying semi-finished bases and adding flavouring. A list of fifteen that changes through the year means somebody is making it from fruit that is in season.

Price is a weak signal in the centre of Florence, where rent decides more than quality. Distance from the Duomo is a better one.

## Where the rules are followed

Carapina is credited by other gelato makers with bringing the covered pozzetti back to Florence and pulling the city's standards up. Sbrino names the farms its milk comes from. Gelateria della Passera keeps a short list and changes it with the season. Badiani, out on Viale dei Mille, is where to try Buontalenti, an egg and cream flavour invented in Florence that most visitors never hear about.

Florence has a market for every purpose, and they are easy to confuse. The food markets are where the city shops; the stalls in between are mostly for visitors.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>For food, go in the morning.</strong> The working food markets close in the early afternoon. Go before eleven, when the stalls are full and the regulars are shopping.</p>
</div>

## Sant'Ambrogio

The neighbourhood market of Santa Croce, in an iron hall with stalls spilling outside. Fruit, vegetables, meat, cheese and fish inside; clothes and household things outside. Monday to Saturday, roughly from seven in the morning to two. The prices are Florentine prices.

Inside there is a counter, Da Rocco, where market workers eat a cheap plate of the day at lunch.

## Mercato Centrale

The big nineteenth-century hall in San Lorenzo. Downstairs is still a working market in the morning: butchers, tripe sellers, cheese and salumi. Upstairs is a food hall with counters for pasta, pizza, wine and more, open all day and into the evening, every day.

## San Lorenzo and Mercato Nuovo

The outdoor stalls around the Mercato Centrale sell leather jackets, bags and souvenirs. Quality varies. Bargaining is expected, and real Florentine leather costs what it costs.

The Mercato Nuovo, the covered loggia with the bronze boar, *il Porcellino*, sells souvenirs. Rub the boar's nose for luck and move on.

## Weekly and monthly markets

On Tuesday mornings a long market of clothes, shoes, food and everything else runs along the avenue of the Cascine park. It is where Florentines go for bargains.

Piazza Santo Spirito hosts a monthly antiques and bric-a-brac market on a Sunday, traditionally the second of the month.

The flea market in Largo Annigoni, near Piazza dei Ciompi, sells second-hand furniture, prints and old things, with a bigger edition at the end of the month.

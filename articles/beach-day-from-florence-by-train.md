Florence is an hour and a bit from the sea, and in summer the regional trains to the coast are full of Florentines with beach bags. You do not need a car.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Viareggio is the easy one.</strong> A regional train from Santa Maria Novella, about an hour and a quarter to an hour and a half, around ten euro. The beach is ten minutes on foot from the station.</p>
</div>

## Viareggio

A long, wide beach backed by a seafront promenade of Liberty-style buildings, with the Apuan Alps rising behind the town. Trains leave Florence often through the day.

The sand is divided into *bagni*, bathing establishments with rows of coloured umbrellas, changing cabins, showers and a bar. You pay for a lounger and umbrella by the day. Free stretches, the *spiaggia libera*, are at the ends of the seafront.

Walk the long pier at the port, the Molo, in the late afternoon. It is where the town goes for its evening stroll.

## Lido di Camaiore

One stop past Viareggio, and quieter. The same long sandy beach, with a more local feel and fewer day-trippers.

## Further down the coast

Livorno has rocky swimming spots along its seafront and is a city with its own food: see our picks there. Further south, San Vincenzo is about two hours from Florence, with a long beach a short walk from the station.

## How to do it

Buy a regional ticket at the station or in the Trenitalia app. Regional fares are fixed and do not need booking; a paper ticket must be validated before you board.

Go early. The trains fill up on weekend mornings in July and August, and the best spots on the free beaches are taken by ten. Come back around six, before the evening rush, or stay for dinner and take a later train.
